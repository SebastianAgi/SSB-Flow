Supervise the Belief, Not the Policy: Simulator-Supervised Belief for Partially-Observed Manipulation

Anonymous Authors

Under review at ICRA 2027

Abstract

How can simulation efficiently teach a visuomotor policy to gather information? Hidden physical properties, such as mass and friction, substantially affect how a robot can optimally manipulate objects. A privileged teacher directly accesses these properties, so its actions generally do not demonstrate how to acquire missing information, creating a mismatch for a partially-observing learner, which does not observe the privileged state during training or deployment. We instead use a simulator to simultaneously supervise a learned approximation to the belief state and train a policy conditioned on that approximation by reinforcement learning. Our method, Simulator-Supervised Belief Flow (SSB-Flow), learns a sampleable posterior over task-relevant physical quantities from interaction history, using high-dimensional image input alongside proprioceptive input. The policy consumes an encoding of posterior samples, allowing it to use estimated uncertainty when selecting actions. We compare SSB-Flow to three student-teacher approaches originally designed to use privileged information, as well as a strong recurrent RL baseline. Across four challenging manipulation tasks, SSB-Flow achieves the highest success rate on every task, averaging 93.1% versus 64.7% for the strongest alternative, an absolute gap of 28.4%. These results support supervising physical-state inference as an effective use of privileged information for partially observed manipulation.

Method

A recurrent encoder summarizes the wrist-camera image, proprioception, and previous action into a history ht. A conditional flow-matching model, supervised by privileged simulator state, samples K beliefs s(1), …, s(K) over task-relevant physical quantities. The samples show what the belief agrees on and what remains uncertain, and they can stay multimodal while the history is ambiguous. A Deep Sets encoder φ maps them to bt for the actor π. The estimator is trained only by Lflow; the encoder and actor are trained only by PPO, and the actor never receives privileged input.

Simulation Benchmark

Three tasks, four variants in total, in which success depends on a hidden object property. The information the agent needs leaves no trace in the environment's persistent state: passive observation cannot reveal it, only the agent's own interaction history can.

Real-Robot Deployment

We deploy Hidden-Color on a Franka FR3 with an 8 cm white cube placed colored face down, so every trial requires a flip. The policy observes a 54 × 96 RGB image from a wrist-mounted camera, proprioception, and its previous action at 20 Hz. SSB-Flow succeeds in 24 of 30 trials; PPO-LSTM, trained under the same randomization, succeeds in 0 of 30.

The policy tips the cube over, checks the newly revealed side, confirms the color (red), and sorts it into the red bin.
